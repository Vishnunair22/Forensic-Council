Because the advances in technology seldom affect the actual principles espoused in the Constitution and the Amendments to it . Consider the internet and the first amendment . The first amendment does n't say anything about the internet ... or does it ? The first amendment says we have freedom of the press , by which it means the printed word ; it does n't matter whether it 's an old - fashioned printing press or a modern digital one ( the ' net ) . It says we have freedom of speech . It does n't matter whether we 're speaking in the public square , over the broadcast airwaves , or in a YouTube video . It says we have the right to petition ~~Congress~~ the government . It does n't matter whether we petition by sending a letter or an email . In those few cases where it might arguably be something that is n't directly covered ( does creating a silent video count as speech or press or something else ? ) the courts generally look at the intent behind the exact letter of the law . That is , it does n't really make much difference whether the silent video is counted as speech or press , because either way it 's protected , and if it 's something else , it 's still something so close that it would make sense to give it the same protection .

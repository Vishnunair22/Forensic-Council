Essentially it comes down to this : you want to create as much lift as possible , for as little drag as possible , using the smallest amount of weight possible . And , of course , it has to be structurally sound . The plane in the picture would probably collapse . The wings are creating drag , so they are all being pushed backward , and those skinny pylons would likely break . On top of that , the drag would be trying to tip the plane backward . Even if it was strong enough , it would n't fly well , if at all . Multiple wings do work , and did for all of WWI , but a more powerful engine , a stronger single wing , and a swept back wing shape are all designed for strength and efficiency . The most efficient design is two long skinny wings , but material strength has n't quite made that possible for commercial use .

I 'm actually taking some fire investigation classes for work in the near future . It 's fascinating stuff really . The biggest variance is the extent of the damage from a fire . A single wide that is burnt to the foundation will be very difficult to determine a starting point or cause , but can still be done based on the extent of charring on the surviving beams . A home that is gutted by a fire is much easier to work with , things such as the charring pattern of the beams can be used to trace where the fire was hottest or coolest , and samples of the materials in that area can be collected to determine if an accelerant is present . The presence of melted or discolored metals can also be used to trace the path of a fire , using the melting and oxidizing points of the various metals to determine where the fire is hottest . Another interesting fact , evidence collected from a fire scene is placed in an airtight container , and the fumes that are released from the material are used to determine if an accelerant is present , and even tell you what sort of accelerant it is . As far as the cause of the fire , once you determine the starting point of the fire , you can deduce the cause of the fire , even without any actual evidence present . I.E. the fire started in the center of the living room - interview the occupants to determine what would have been in that area , work from there . A quick word on electrical heaters / radiators .... the manufacturer has a minimum open area recommendation around the heater - stick to that , keep flammables away from them , and for fucks sake do n't use them as a table .

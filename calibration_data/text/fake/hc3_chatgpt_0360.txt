D.O. stands for Doctor of Osteopathic Medicine. It is a professional doctoral degree in medicine that is awarded to individuals who have completed a program of study in osteopathic medicine at an accredited college or university. Osteopathic medicine is a system of medical care that focuses on the whole person and the role that the musculoskeletal system plays in health and disease. D.O.s receive training in all of the same core medical subjects as M.D.s (Doctors of Medicine), but they also receive additional training in osteopathic manipulative treatment (OMT), a hands-on approach to diagnosis and treatment that involves moving and manipulating muscles, bones, and other tissues in the body. D.O.s are licensed to practice medicine in all 50 states and are fully qualified to prescribe medications and perform surgery. They work in a variety of medical settings, including hospitals, clinics, and private practices, and they can specialize in any area of medicine.

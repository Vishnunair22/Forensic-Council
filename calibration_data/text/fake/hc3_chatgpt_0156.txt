A basis point is a unit of measurement used to express the percentage change in the value or rate of a financial instrument. It is equal to 1/100th of 1% or 0.01%. So, if something increases by 100 basis points, it has increased by 1%. If something decreases by 50 basis points, it has decreased by 0.5%.\n\nFor example, if the interest rate on a loan is 6% and it increases by 25 basis points, the new interest rate would be 6.25%. If the interest rate on a loan is 5% and it decreases by 50 basis points, the new interest rate would be 4.5%.\n\nBasis points are commonly used in the financial industry to express changes in interest rates, bond yields, and other financial indicators. They are used because they allow for precise and concise communication of small percentage changes.

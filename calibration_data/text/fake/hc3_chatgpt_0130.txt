Antibodies are proteins produced by the immune system in response to the presence of a foreign substance, such as a virus or bacteria. They can be used in a variety of ways, including:\n\nDiagnostic testing: Antibodies can be used to detect the presence of a specific virus or other pathogen in a sample, such as a blood or tissue sample. This is often done using a technique called an ELISA (enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay) or a rapid diagnostic test.\n\nTreatment of infections: Some infections can be treated with antibodies that are produced naturally by the body, or with artificially produced antibodies called monoclonal antibodies. These treatments can help to neutralize the pathogen and reduce the severity of the infection.\n\nResearch: Antibodies can be used in research to study the structure and function of specific proteins or to identify the presence of specific proteins in a sample.\n\nAllergy testing: Antibodies can be used to test for allergies by measuring the level of antibodies to specific allergens in a person's blood.\n\nImmunotherapy: Antibodies can be used to stimulate the immune system to attack cancer cells or to reduce the activity of certain immune system cells that contribute to autoimmune diseases.

Blood urea nitrogen (BUN) is a measure of the amount of urea present in the blood. Urea is a waste product that is produced when proteins are broken down in the body. It is normally excreted in the urine, but high levels of urea in the blood can indicate kidney dysfunction or other health problems.\n\nBUN is usually measured in milligrams per deciliter (mg/dL). Normal BUN levels are usually between 7 and 20 mg/dL. However, the normal range can vary depending on a person's age, sex, and other factors. High BUN levels (greater than 20 mg/dL) can be a sign of kidney problems, such as kidney damage or failure. Low BUN levels (less than 7 mg/dL) can indicate malnutrition or a deficiency in protein intake.\n\nBUN is typically used in conjunction with other tests, such as a creatinine test, to help diagnose and monitor kidney function. It is important to note that BUN levels can be affected by factors other than kidney function, such as diet, hydration status, and certain medications, so it is important to interpret BUN results in the context of a person's overall clinical picture.\n

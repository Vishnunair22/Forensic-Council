Thunder is the sound that is produced by lightning. When lightning occurs, it creates a sudden and powerful surge of electricity that travels through the air. This surge of electricity is accompanied by a rapid expansion of the surrounding air, which creates a shock wave. The shock wave travels through the air and eventually reaches the ground, where it can be heard as thunder.\n\nThe intensity of the thunder sound is determined by the intensity of the lightning and the distance from the lightning strike. Lightning that is closer to the observer will produce louder thunder, while lightning that is farther away will produce softer thunder. The speed of sound is about 343 meters per second (763 miles per hour), so if you see lightning and then hear thunder a few seconds later, you can calculate how far away the lightning strike was by counting the number of seconds between the flash of light and the sound of thunder and multiplying that number by 343 meters per second. For example, if you count 5 seconds between the lightning and the thunder, then the lightning strike was about 1,715 meters (1 mile) away.

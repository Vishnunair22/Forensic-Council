Blood urea nitrogen (BUN) is a measure of the amount of urea in the blood. Urea is a waste product that is produced by the liver as it breaks down proteins. It is eliminated from the body through the kidneys, which filter it out of the blood and excrete it in the urine.\n\nBUN is used as a marker of kidney function. When the kidneys are functioning properly, they are able to remove urea from the blood and excrete it in the urine. If the kidneys are not functioning properly, the level of urea in the blood may rise, indicating kidney dysfunction.\n\nBUN is typically measured in milligrams per deciliter (mg/dL). Normal BUN levels in adults range from 6 to 20 mg/dL. Elevated BUN levels may indicate kidney disease or other conditions that affect kidney function, such as dehydration, urinary tract obstruction, or liver disease.\n\nIt's important to note that BUN levels can be affected by factors other than kidney function, such as diet, medication use, and underlying medical conditions. Therefore, BUN levels should be interpreted in the context of a person's overall clinical presentation and other laboratory tests.

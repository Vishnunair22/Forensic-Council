Lobbying groups, also known as interest groups or special interest groups, are organizations that seek to influence public policy and legislation. They may represent a specific industry, cause, or ideology and work to promote their interests to lawmakers and decision-makers. Lobbying groups may use a variety of tactics, such as issuing policy statements, conducting research, organizing grassroots campaigns, and meeting with legislators, to advocate for their positions. Some examples of lobbying groups include trade associations, labor unions, and advocacy organizations.

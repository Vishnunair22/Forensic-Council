A stanza is a group of lines in a poem that is set off by a blank line or indentation. It is similar to a paragraph in prose, in that it is a unit of organization within the poem. Stanzas can have a fixed number of lines or a flexible number of lines, and they can follow a specific rhyme scheme or be unrhymed. The word "stanza" comes from the Italian word for "room," and it is used to describe a section of a poem that is set apart from the rest of the poem in a way that creates a sense of separation or enclosure. Stanzas are often used to structure a poem and to create a sense of structure and form within the poem.

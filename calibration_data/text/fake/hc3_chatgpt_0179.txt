The Myers-Briggs Type Indicator (MBTI) is a personality assessment tool that helps individuals understand their personality preferences and how they perceive and make decisions. It is based on the psychological theories of Carl Jung and was developed by Katherine Briggs and her daughter, Isabel Briggs Myers.\n\nThe MBTI is a self-report questionnaire that consists of a series of questions designed to measure an individual's preferences in four areas:\n\nExtraversion (E) versus Introversion (I): This dimension measures how an individual tends to direct their energy and attention outward (extraversion) or inward (introversion).\n\nSensing (S) versus Intuition (N): This dimension measures how an individual tends to process information and make decisions based on either sensory data (sensing) or abstract concepts and patterns (intuition).\n\nThinking (T) versus Feeling (F): This dimension measures how an individual tends to make decisions based on logical analysis (thinking) or personal values (feeling).\n\nJudging (J) versus Perceiving (P): This dimension measures how an individual tends to approach the external world, either with a planned and structured approach (judging) or a flexible and adaptable approach (perceiving).\n\nThe MBTI categorizes individuals into one of 16 personality types based on their scores in these four dimensions. The different personality types are designated by a combination of four letters, such as INTJ (introverted, intuitive, thinking, and judging) or ESFP (extraverted, sensing, feeling, and perceiving).\n\nThe MBTI is often used in a variety of settings, including education, career counseling, and leadership development. It is a popular tool but has also been criticized for its lack of empirical support and its limited ability to predict behavior.\n

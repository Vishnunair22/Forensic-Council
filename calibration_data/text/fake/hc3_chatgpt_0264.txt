The Cash and Carry policy was a provision of the Lend-Lease Act of 1941, which allowed the United States to provide military aid to its allies during World War II. Under the Cash and Carry policy, the United States would sell or lease military equipment, food, and other supplies to countries that were fighting against the Axis powers, provided that these countries paid for the goods in cash and arranged for their own transportation. The Cash and Carry policy was implemented as a way for the United States to provide assistance to its allies without becoming directly involved in the war. It was eventually replaced by the Lend-Lease Act, which allowed the United States to provide military aid to its allies on more favorable terms.

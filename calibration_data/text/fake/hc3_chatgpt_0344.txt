Lyme disease is an infectious disease caused by the bacterium Borrelia burgdorferi. It is transmitted to humans through the bite of infected ticks. The most common symptoms of Lyme disease include:\n\nRash: A distinctive bull's-eye rash, also known as erythema migrans, often appears at the site of the tick bite. It may be warm to the touch and may appear several days to several weeks after the tick bite.\n\nFlu-like symptoms: People with Lyme disease may experience symptoms such as fever, chills, fatigue, muscle aches, and joint pain.\n\nNeurological symptoms: In some cases, Lyme disease can cause neurological symptoms such as meningitis (inflammation of the membranes surrounding the brain and spinal cord), facial paralysis, and numbness or tingling in the hands or feet.\n\nHeart problems: Rarely, Lyme disease can cause heart problems such as irregular heartbeat or heart palpitations.\n\nIf left untreated, Lyme disease can progress and cause more serious complications such as arthritis, nerve damage, and cognitive problems. Early diagnosis and treatment of Lyme disease are important to prevent these complications.\n\nIt's important to note that not everyone who is infected with the bacteria that causes Lyme disease will develop symptoms, and not everyone who has symptoms will develop all of the above-mentioned side effects. If you think you may have been exposed to ticks and are experiencing any of the above-mentioned symptoms, it is important to consult a healthcare provider for further evaluation and treatment.

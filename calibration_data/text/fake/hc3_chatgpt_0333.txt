Blood urea nitrogen (BUN) is a measure of the amount of urea in the blood. Urea is a waste product that is produced when proteins are broken down in the body. It is removed from the body through the kidneys, which filter the blood and excrete excess urea and other waste products in the urine.\n\nA blood test for BUN measures the amount of urea in the blood and can be used to evaluate how well the kidneys are functioning. An elevated BUN level may indicate kidney damage or dysfunction, while a low BUN level may be a sign of liver disease or malnutrition.\n\nBUN is typically measured in milligrams per deciliter (mg/dL). Normal BUN levels in adults are typically between 7 and 20 mg/dL, but the normal range may vary depending on the specific laboratory that performs the test.

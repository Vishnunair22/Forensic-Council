Seasons are caused by the tilt of the Earth's axis as it orbits the sun. The Earth's axis is tilted about 23.5 degrees relative to its orbit around the sun. This tilt causes the direct rays of the sun to hit the Earth at different angles throughout the year, resulting in the different seasons.\n\nIn the Northern Hemisphere, the tilt of the Earth's axis causes the direct rays of the sun to hit the Earth more directly in the summer, resulting in longer days and warmer temperatures. In the winter, the direct rays of the sun hit the Earth at a lower angle, causing shorter days and cooler temperatures. In the Southern Hemisphere, the opposite is true, with shorter days and cooler temperatures in the summer and longer days and warmer temperatures in the winter.\n\nSeasons also vary based on the location of the Earth in its orbit around the sun. When the Earth is farther from the sun, it is cooler, and when it is closer to the sun, it is warmer. This variation in the Earth's distance from the sun also contributes to the changing seasons.

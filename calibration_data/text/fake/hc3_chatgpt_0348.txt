Richard Nixon referred to a group of Americans who supported his policies and political agenda as the "silent majority." This term was coined by Nixon during his presidential campaign in 1969, and he used it to describe a group of people who he believed supported his views on issues such as law and order, the Vietnam War, and social and cultural issues, but who were not vocal or active in expressing their opinions. Nixon argued that this silent majority was the true majority of the American people, and that their views were being drowned out by more vocal and visible minority groups. He appealed to the silent majority during his campaign and throughout his presidency, and used their support to justify and advance his policies.

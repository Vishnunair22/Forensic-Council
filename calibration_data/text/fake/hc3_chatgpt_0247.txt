Thunder is the sound produced by lightning. When lightning occurs, a channel of hot plasma (a gas made up of ions, or charged particles) is created in the air. This plasma channel can be several miles long and extremely hot, reaching temperatures of up to 50,000 degrees Fahrenheit. As the plasma channel cools, it contracts, creating a shock wave that travels through the air. When this shock wave reaches our ears, we hear it as thunder. The loudness of the thunder depends on the distance between the lightning and the observer, as well as the intensity of the lightning.

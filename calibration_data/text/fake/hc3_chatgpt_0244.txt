Vitamin A is a nutrient that is essential for maintaining healthy vision, skin, and mucous membranes. It also plays a role in immune function, bone growth, and reproduction. Vitamin A is a fat-soluble vitamin, which means that it is stored in the body's fatty tissue and liver.\n\nThere are two forms of vitamin A: retinol and carotenoids. Retinol is found in animal-derived foods such as liver, eggs, and milk, while carotenoids are found in plant-based foods such as fruits and vegetables. The body can convert some carotenoids, such as beta-carotene, into vitamin A.\n\nVitamin A deficiency can lead to vision problems, including night blindness, and can also impair immune function, leading to an increased risk of infections. It is important to get enough vitamin A through your diet or through supplements, as deficiency can have serious health consequences. However, it is also important not to consume too much vitamin A, as high levels of the vitamin can be toxic.

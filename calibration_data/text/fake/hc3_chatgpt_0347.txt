Richard Nixon referred to the "silent majority" as a group of people who supported his policies and goals but did not speak out or participate in public demonstrations or protests. Nixon used the term to contrast this group with the minority of vocal activists and protesters who opposed his policies, particularly during the Vietnam War. In a November 3, 1969 speech, Nixon said: "And so tonight—to you, the great silent majority of my fellow Americans—I ask for your support." The phrase became associated with Nixon and was widely used during his presidency to describe his political base.

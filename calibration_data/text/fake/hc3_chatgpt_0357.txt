Stocks and bonds are two types of securities that represent ownership in a company or a loan made to a government or corporation, respectively.\n\nStocks, also known as equities, represent ownership in a company and entitle the holder to a share of the company's profits and assets. When you buy a stock, you are buying a small ownership stake in the company. If the company does well and its value increases, the value of your stock may also increase, and you may be able to sell your stock for a profit. On the other hand, if the company does poorly, the value of your stock may decrease.\n\nBonds, on the other hand, represent a loan made to a government or corporation. When you buy a bond, you are lending money to the issuer in exchange for interest payments and the return of principal at a later date. The issuer of the bond is obligated to pay the bondholder a specified rate of interest during the life of the bond and to repay the principal, or face value, of the bond when it matures. The value of a bond can fluctuate based on a number of factors, including the creditworthiness of the issuer and market interest rates.\n\nBoth stocks and bonds can be bought and sold on financial markets, such as stock exchanges. They are often used as investments, with the goal of generating income or capital appreciation.

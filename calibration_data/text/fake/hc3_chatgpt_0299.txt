Osteonecrosis of the jaw, also known as avascular necrosis of the jaw or jawbone death, is a condition that occurs when the blood supply to the jawbone is disrupted, causing the bone tissue to die. This can lead to the breakdown and collapse of the affected area of the jaw, resulting in severe pain and difficulty eating.\n\nOsteonecrosis of the jaw is most often caused by long-term use of certain medications, such as bisphosphonates, which are used to treat osteoporosis and other bone disorders. It can also be caused by radiation therapy to the head and neck, or as a result of other underlying medical conditions.\n\nSymptoms of osteonecrosis of the jaw may include swelling and redness in the affected area, pain or numbness in the jaw, difficulty eating or speaking, and loose teeth. Treatment for osteonecrosis of the jaw may include medications to control pain and inflammation, surgery to remove dead bone tissue, or a combination of both. In severe cases, the affected jawbone may need to be replaced with a bone graft or artificial implant.

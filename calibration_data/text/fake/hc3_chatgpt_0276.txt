The Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA) is a federal law that requires covered employers to provide eligible employees with unpaid, job-protected leave for certain family and medical reasons. The FMLA applies to employers with 50 or more employees, and employees who have worked for the employer for at least 12 months and at least 1,250 hours in the past 12 months are eligible to take FMLA leave.\n\nUnder the FMLA, eligible employees are entitled to take up to 12 weeks of unpaid leave in a 12-month period for certain reasons, including:\n\nThe birth, adoption, or foster placement of a child\nThe serious illness of the employee or a family member\nThe need to care for a family member with a serious illness\nA qualifying exigency arising from the employee's or a family member's military service\nThe employee's own serious health condition that makes them unable to perform their job duties\nWhile on FMLA leave, the employer is required to maintain the employee's group health insurance coverage, and upon returning from leave, the employee must be restored to their original position or an equivalent position with the same pay, benefits, and working conditions.\n\nIt's important to note that the FMLA only provides unpaid leave, and some states have their own family and medical leave laws that may provide additional protections, such as paid leave. Additionally, some employers may have their own policies that provide paid or unpaid leave beyond what is required by the FMLA.

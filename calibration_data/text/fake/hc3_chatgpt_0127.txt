Atherosclerotic heart disease is a type of cardiovascular disease that is characterized by the buildup of plaque in the walls of the arteries. This plaque is made up of fatty deposits, cholesterol, and other substances that accumulate over time. As the plaque builds up, it narrows the arteries and makes it more difficult for blood to flow through them. This can lead to a number of serious health problems, including heart attack, stroke, and angina (chest pain). Atherosclerotic heart disease is often referred to as "hardening of the arteries" or "coronary artery disease." It is a common condition that affects many people and is a leading cause of death worldwide.

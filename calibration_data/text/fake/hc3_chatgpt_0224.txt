Lean manufacturing is a production methodology that aims to minimize waste and maximize efficiency in the manufacturing process. The goal of lean manufacturing is to eliminate activities that do not add value to the product, such as unnecessary transportation, excess inventory, and unnecessary motion. This approach was developed by Toyota Production System (TPS), a system of manufacturing that was developed by the Toyota Motor Corporation in the mid-20th century. TPS is also known as "lean manufacturing" or simply "lean."\n\nThe principles of lean manufacturing are based on the idea that value is created through a series of interconnected processes, and that waste can be reduced by identifying and eliminating non-value-adding activities in these processes. Lean manufacturing focuses on continuous improvement, or "kaizen," and encourages the involvement of all employees in the identification and elimination of waste.\n\nSome of the key principles of lean manufacturing include:\n\nIdentifying the value that a customer is willing to pay for\nIdentifying the value stream for each product, including all the steps necessary to create value\nCreating flow in the value stream by eliminating waste and reducing variability\nPulling products through the value stream in response to customer demand\nContinuously improving the value stream to increase efficiency and reduce waste.\nLean manufacturing has been widely adopted by manufacturers around the world and is considered a key element of the modern manufacturing landscape. It has been credited with helping companies reduce costs, improve quality, and increase competitiveness.

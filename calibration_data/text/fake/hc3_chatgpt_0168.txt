Shiva is a period of mourning in the Jewish tradition that takes place after the death of a close relative. During Shiva, mourners typically observe certain customs and rituals to honor the deceased and provide support for one another.\n\nHere are some common customs and rituals that are often observed during Shiva:\n\nMourners typically sit on low stools or benches and receive visitors who come to offer condolences.\n\nIt is customary for mourners to abstain from work, leisure activities, and other distractions during Shiva.\n\nMourners may recite the traditional Mourner's Kaddish, a prayer that praises God and expresses the belief in the eventual coming of the Messiah.\n\nMourners may also recite other traditional prayers and engage in Torah study as a way to find comfort and meaning during this difficult time.\n\nIt is also common for friends and family members to bring food to the mourners during Shiva, as they may not have the energy or inclination to prepare meals for themselves.\n\nIt is important to note that the specific customs and rituals observed during Shiva can vary somewhat depending on the individual and their community. It is always best to respect the wishes and traditions of the mourners and their family during this time of grieving.

D.O. stands for Doctor of Osteopathic Medicine. It is a professional doctoral degree for physicians and surgeons offered by medical schools in the United States. D.O.s are licensed to practice medicine and surgery in all 50 states and are trained to focus on the whole person, rather than just the symptoms of a disease. They use a hands-on approach called osteopathic manipulative medicine (OMM) to diagnose and treat illness, and they also prescribe medications and perform surgery. D.O.s are trained to consider the body's structure and function and how they relate to each other and to the overall health of the patient. They work in a variety of medical settings, including hospitals, clinics, and private practice.

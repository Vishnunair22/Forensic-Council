In the United States, the term "middle class" refers to a socio-economic group that falls between the upper and lower classes. The middle class is often characterized by its position as the social group with the greatest economic stability and the highest level of education. It is generally defined as those who have a moderate amount of wealth and income, and who hold intermediate social status in society.\n\nThere is no specific income range that defines the middle class, as the concept of what constitutes the middle class can vary depending on the context and the specific criteria being used. Some people define the middle class based on income, while others use factors such as education, occupation, or lifestyle as indicators of middle-class status. In general, however, the middle class is often thought of as those who have a relatively high level of income and wealth, but who are not among the highest earners or most affluent members of society.\n\nIt is worth noting that the concept of the middle class is often used in a political or economic context, and it is sometimes used to describe a group that is seen as being vital to the stability and prosperity of a society. Some people argue that the middle class is the backbone of a healthy economy, as it is made up of consumers who have the means to make purchases and contribute to economic growth. Others believe that the middle class is threatened by factors such as rising inequality, stagnant wages, and the growing influence of the wealthy, and that policies and programs should be implemented to support and protect this group.

Lobbying groups, also known as interest groups or special interest groups, are organizations that attempt to influence public policy and legislation by advocating for the interests of a particular group or cause. These groups often lobby government officials, such as members of Congress or state legislators, to support policies that align with their goals. Lobbying groups can be found at the local, state, and national levels, and they can represent a wide range of interests, including business, labor, environmental, social justice, and more. Some common lobbying tactics include meeting with legislators, testifying at hearings, issuing press releases and other communications, and participating in grassroots campaigns to mobilize public support.

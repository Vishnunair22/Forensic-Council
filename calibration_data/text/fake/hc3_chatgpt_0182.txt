Ground pepper, also known as black pepper, comes from the small, round, dried fruit of the pepper plant, which is native to tropical regions of Asia. The fruit, known as a peppercorn, is harvested when it is ripe and then dried in the sun or by mechanical means. The outer layer of the peppercorn is then removed to reveal the inner, black seed. The seeds are then ground into a fine powder to produce ground pepper. Black pepper is the most widely used type of pepper in the world and is used in a variety of dishes to add flavor and spice. Other types of pepper, such as white pepper and green pepper, are also produced from the pepper plant, but are processed differently. White pepper is made by removing the outer layer of the peppercorn before it is ground, while green pepper is made from unripe peppercorns that are pickled or dried.

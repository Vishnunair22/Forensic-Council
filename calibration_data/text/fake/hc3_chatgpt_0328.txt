World War II was caused by a variety of factors, including longstanding tensions between European powers, the rise of aggressive dictatorships in Nazi Germany and Italy, and the failure of international diplomacy to prevent the outbreak of conflict.\n\nOne of the main causes of World War II was the Treaty of Versailles, which ended World War I and imposed harsh penalties on Germany. The treaty humiliated and weakened Germany, and many Germans believed that it was unfair. The treaty also led to the rise of Adolf Hitler and the Nazi Party, which promised to restore Germany's pride and power.\n\nHitler and the Nazi Party came to power in 1933 and began a campaign of aggressive expansion, annexing Austria and parts of Czechoslovakia and Poland. Hitler also signed a non-aggression pact with the Soviet Union, which allowed him to invade Poland without fear of Soviet intervention.\n\nOther factors that contributed to the outbreak of World War II include the rise of aggressive dictatorships in Italy and Japan, which also sought to expand their territories, and the failure of international diplomacy to prevent the outbreak of conflict. The world had not fully recovered from World War I, and many nations were unwilling to take strong action to prevent the rise of aggressive regimes or to stop them from expanding their territories.

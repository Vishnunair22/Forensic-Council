Thunder is the sound produced by lightning. When lightning occurs, a channel of hot plasma (a type of ionized gas) is created in the air. This plasma channel is very hot and expands rapidly, creating a shock wave. The shock wave travels through the air as a sound wave, and when it reaches your ears, you hear it as thunder.\n\nThe intensity of the thunder depends on the intensity of the lightning and the distance between the lightning and the observer. Thunder is typically much louder than the sound of the shock wave created by the lightning, because the thunder is created by the shock wave traveling through the air. The sound of the thunder can be heard from much further away than the light from the lightning, because the speed of sound is much slower than the speed of light.

"Freedom Summer" was a campaign in the United States in the summer of 1964 to register as many African American voters as possible in Mississippi. The campaign was organized by civil rights groups, including the Student Nonviolent Coordinating Committee (SNCC) and the Congress of Racial Equality (CORE), and was supported by many white volunteers and civil rights activists.\n\nDuring Freedom Summer, hundreds of volunteers, both black and white, traveled to Mississippi to help African Americans register to vote. Many of these volunteers faced violence and intimidation, and several were killed, including James Chaney, Andrew Goodman, and Michael Schwerner. Despite the dangers, the campaign was successful in registering thousands of African American voters in Mississippi and helped to pave the way for the passage of the Voting Rights Act of 1965, which prohibited racial discrimination in voting.

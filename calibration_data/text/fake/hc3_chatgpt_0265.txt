The Lend-Lease Act, also known as the "Cash and Carry" Act, was a program enacted by the United States government during World War II that provided military aid to the Allied powers. It allowed the United States to provide military equipment, food, and other supplies to countries fighting against the Axis powers without requiring immediate payment. Instead, the recipient countries were required to pay for the supplies after the war ended or to provide other forms of compensation, such as the transfer of military bases or other assets. The Lend-Lease Act was a significant tool for the United States to support the Allied war effort and played a key role in the eventual victory over the Axis powers.

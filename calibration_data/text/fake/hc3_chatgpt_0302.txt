The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus is a theorem that establishes the connection between the concept of a definite integral and the concept of a derivative. It has two parts: the First Fundamental Theorem of Calculus and the Second Fundamental Theorem of Calculus.\n\nThe First Fundamental Theorem of Calculus states that if f is a continuous function defined on a closed interval [a,b], then the definite integral of f from a to b can be computed as the limit of a sum, called a Riemann sum. This theorem allows us to evaluate definite integrals using a process called integration by substitution or integration by parts.\n\nThe Second Fundamental Theorem of Calculus states that if F is an antiderivative of f on an interval [a,b] (that is, F'(x) = f(x) for all x in [a,b]), then the definite integral of f from a to b is given by F(b) - F(a). This theorem allows us to find the indefinite integral of a function by finding an antiderivative of the function.\n\nThe Fundamental Theorem of Calculus has many important applications in mathematics and the sciences. It is used to solve problems involving the accumulation of quantities, the average value of a function, and the area under a curve. It is also used in the study of optimization problems and in the solution of differential equations.

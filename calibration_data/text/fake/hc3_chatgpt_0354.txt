In poetry, a stanza is a group of lines that form a unit within a poem. A stanza typically has a set pattern of meter and rhyme, and it often has a specific length. Some common types of stanzas include the couplet, which consists of two lines; the triplet, which consists of three lines; and the quatrain, which consists of four lines. The term "stanza" comes from the Italian word "stanza," which means "room," and it is used to describe a unit of poetry because each stanza is like a separate room within the poem.
